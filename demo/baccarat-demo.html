    How a hand plays

    Cards count Ace = 1, 2–9 face value, 10 and face cards = 0. Only the last digit of the sum counts, so 7 + 8 = 15 = 5. Two cards go to Player and two to Banker, dealt alternately. A two-card 8 or 9 is a natural and ends the hand. Otherwise Player acts first: 0–5 draws, 6–7 stands. Banker then follows the table below, which depends on whether Player drew and what Player's third card was.

    The Banker bet is the best bet on the table under every rule set, and EZ Baccarat makes it the cheapest of all: the casino keeps its edge by pushing the three-card 7 instead of charging commission. Dragon 7 and Panda 8 are the only bets that can turn positive, and only when the shoe runs rich in the cards they need. The Coach tracks the Dragon 7 count for you.

    Casino procedure, as simulated

    Eight standard 52-card decks, 416 cards in all, are shuffled together and dealt from a shoe. That is the configuration at the vast majority of land-based and online punto banco tables, and it is the default here; a 6-deck shoe (312 cards) can be selected under the table, and the Coach and the payout table switch to the exact 6-deck house edges when it is. A cut card is placed 14 cards from the end. The first card is turned up and its value (face cards count 10) says how many cards are burned. When the cut card comes out during a hand, that hand finishes, one more hand is dealt, and the shoe is replaced. Third cards are placed sideways, as the dealer does. Table limits at the $10 mini-baccarat table: Player and Banker $10–$5,000, Tie $10–$1,000, Dragon 7, Panda 8 and the pairs $5–$500. Commission (classic rule) is taken from each winning Banker payout.

    The Dragon 7 count: 4, 5, 6 and 7 count −1 as they leave the shoe; 8 and 9 count +2; everything else 0. Divide the running count by the decks left to get the true count. At a true count of 4 or more, the Dragon 7 bet is worth more than it costs. Below that, it is a 7.6% tax.
